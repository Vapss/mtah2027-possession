Possession as the Last Controlled Ball:
a Rule-Based Chain for BAR–ATH, Decided Without the Ground Truth

More than a Hack 2027 — Phase 1: Ball Possession (Metrica Sports × FC Barcelona) · Technical document

Team {{TEAM}} · Repository: {{REPO}} · Submission SHA-256 {{SHA}} · {{DATE}}

Abstract. We label each of the 199,319 frames of the tactical video with the team of the last controlled ball. A frame is a control when the ball is seen inside the pitch, moves no faster than 5 m/s and has a player within 1.5 m with no rival as close. A rival control does not change possession while the team in possession can also reach the ball (a duel), and between controls the state is carried. Dead ball is the union of stoppages tagged by Smart Tagging (ball out → restart) and stoppages read from the players' speeds by a logistic detector trained only on seven open DFL matches. A tagged restart hands the ball to the team that takes it; an untagged stoppage ends with the first accepted control. Every threshold comes from physics, from the laws of the game, or from external data. After the organisers clarified the rules, we went back over every past selection. We deleted every gate that read the public ground truth, re-applied our own written rules, and accepted only what external or label-free evidence still supported. The chain is deterministic numpy/pandas code that rebuilds the submitted file byte for byte in about 18 s.

1. Task, metric and the rules we hold ourselves to

Each frame gets H (home in possession), A (away), D (dead ball) or X (no answer). The ranking metric, spellF1, matches whole possessions (maximal H/A runs) one to one with the truth by temporal IoU, averaged over thresholds 0.3–0.7. macroF1 only breaks ties. A method that flickers between teams can be right on most frames and still score near zero, so every design choice below is about possession structure, not per-frame accuracy. The only labelled data for this match is a public 5-minute window: 17 possessions, recorded with the ball detected in 88% of its frames, against 59% over the whole match.

The organisers' clarification takes precedence over all other documents, and we apply its strictest reading:

  • No choice rests on the ground truth or the video. No constant, rule or decision to include a component of the submitted chain may rest on the public ground truth, on any instrument scored against it, or on the video. Anything that needs tuning is tuned on external data. Scoring a chain that is already decided is an automatic check. Choosing between variants with that score is not.
  • No human labels for this match. No human-produced data about this match is used, not even to validate.
  • No output checked against the video. The delivered chain does not read the video, and no constant was chosen by looking at it.
  • No match data in hosted AI services. Provided data must never reach a hosted model. §9 states what the repository can show and what it cannot.

Every decision is logged in docs/DECISIONS.md (cited as Dnn), with its rule committed before the measurement it depends on.

3. Method

3.1 Control and carrying

The challenge defines a change of possession as the other team gaining controlled possession. While the ball is contested or loose, it stays with the last team. We encode that sentence directly. With dT(t) the distance from the ball to team T's nearest player and v(t) the ball's speed, frame t is a control of team T when

ball seen in pitch∧dT≤1.5 m∧dT<dT‾∧v≤5 m/s.

The constants are physical and were fixed before any sweep (D29). 1.5 m is a player's reach plus tracking noise. 5 m/s is a fast dribble, while a pass travels at 10 m/s or more. An unknown speed, a tie or a ball outside the pitch never counts as a control: every uncertain case fails closed. Possession is the team of the last control, carried within each half. A change is dated to the rival's first control frame (dwell = 1). Frames before a half's first control are X, which the scorer back-fills.

3.2 The duel rule

A rival control changes possession only if the team in possession has nobody within 1.5 m of the ball. When both teams can play the ball it is a duel, and possession stays where it was (Fig. 2b). This is the challenge's own definition: "in phases where the ball is contested or loose, possession stays with the last team that had it". It is also the contested-ball rule of Vidal-Codina et al. (2022). It uses the radius the engine already has and adds no constant (D34, D45).

3.3 Dead ball: tagged stoppages ∪ players' stoppages

Tagged stoppages (D30). Each tagged restart closes a stoppage. The stoppage opens at the last episode of the ball seen outside the pitch in the preceding 40 s (120 s after a goal). Otherwise it covers the tag window only. Short episodes count: 69% of the stoppages opened by an episode shorter than 1 s end in a throw-in, corner or goal kick, so they are real outs (D45, test B).

Players' stoppages (D36, D45). When the referee stops play, nobody runs. A ridge logistic regression on 29 order-invariant features flags dead frames. The features are the median and 90th-percentile speed of all tracked players over windows from ±1 s to 30 s before and after the frame, the distance to the nearest corner, the number of players on a touchline and in the boxes, and the spread of the field. It never reads the ball or player identities, so it survives ball gaps and slot swaps. It was trained only on the seven DFL matches, and its weights are embedded in the code. Its decoder settings were chosen on DFL with our match's missing-ball pattern overlaid: probability above 0.5, live gaps under 1 s closed, dead runs under 4 s dropped. Leave-one-match-out on DFL gives precision / recall / accuracy 0.885 / 0.884 / 0.895, close to the 92% that Lang et al. (2022) report with player identities. Where fewer than 8 players are tracked the detector abstains: losing players is not evidence that they stopped.

3.4 Restarts

After a stoppage there is always a clear possessor. At each tagged restart whose team is known, the decoder forces that team's possession, unless the tracking has already seen an accepted, non-duel control inside the tag window. An observation beats an estimated restart frame (D30, D32, D35); 17 restarts are forced this way. If two restarts on one frame name different teams, neither is forced. A stoppage that no tag closes ends with the team of the first accepted control after it (D45); this happens 37 times in the match.

4. Handling missing and noisy tracking

The ball is absent in 41% of graded frames, and in 19.9–36.9% of some 5-minute blocks. We never infer possession from an absent ball, and gaps carry the last state. Speed is never computed across a gap. Controls fail closed under any doubt. Dead ball is read from the 22 players, who are tracked far more reliably than the ball. The restarts come from Smart Tagging, not from the ball. We tried filling ball gaps from formation, from a self-supervised imputation, from Smart Tagging and from a video detector. None had admissible evidence in favour (§6).

5. Deciding without the ground truth

5.1 Protocol

Each research lane committed its variants, gates and a prior order before measuring, and results were appended without editing the rule. Our earlier gates included the public window, and a "bench" that transplants missing-ball patterns onto that window. Both read the ground truth. After the clarification the team decided that the window and the bench may check but never choose (D44). An independent read-only audit then traced the provenance of every constant and inclusion decision (D45).

We then re-applied every written selection rule, deleting each gate that reads the ground truth or a figure derived from it, such as its 3.4 possessions per minute. A component stays only with admissible evidence in favour:

  • the challenge's own definition of possession, the laws of the game, physics or the literature;
  • external data whose rights are verified: DFL/IDSSE (CC-BY 4.0);
  • label-free checks: block structure, and agreement with Smart Tagging phases.

Re-applying the rules changed one component and confirmed the rest (Table 3).

The new chain passes the DFL-derived block limits. Dead ball covers 48.2% of the halves, against a ceiling of 51.7%. The busiest block reaches 70.8%, against 88.6%, and no block is left without possessions. The chain gives 3.11 possessions per minute, and agreement with Smart Tagging rises from 77.1% to 77.7%.

Declared exposure. We knew before this review that the logistic variants scored higher on the public window. The switch to W3 follows mechanically from deleting the ground-truth gate and applying the written prior, not from that number. Even so, that exposure means the window's score for W3 is not independent evidence. The external justification of 1.5 m / 5 m/s / one frame beyond physics is still open and tracked by the team (issue #63).

7. Limitations and what we would do with more time

  • Timing near a touch. Our ball's noise, about 1 m at a slow ball, makes an early touch as often false as true. This is where the remaining spellF1 is, and no rule we tried recovers it without inventing changes.
  • Dead-ball share. 48% of the halves is D, inside DFL's 41–52% per match. The organisers' definition of a stoppage may be narrower, and we cannot check that without the ground truth.
  • Long ball gaps. In the worst blocks (under 30% ball), possession is carried through gaps of over a minute. Only a better ball detector that generalises across halves would help.
  • No admissible external judge for possession changes. DFL's truth is touch-based and finer than the challenge's, and Metrica's data has no declared licence. A licensed corpus with the challenge's control definition would be the missing judge.

8. Reproduction

Python 3.12.3 with numpy and pandas only in the labelling path, pinned in uv.lock. No network, no seeds, no training at run time.

uv sync --locked --no-dev --inexact                  # = make install
export MTAH_DATA_DIR=/path/to/bundle                 # licensed data, never in the repository
python tools/engine.py --build runs/engine --submission submissions/engine.csv   # = make engine
python evaluation/validate_submission.py submissions/engine.csv
python tools/repro.py --against submissions/engine.csv                           # = make repro

repro.py builds the chain twice from scratch and compares the bytes. The delivered file's SHA-256 is {{SHA}}. On an Intel i9-13980HX laptop (16 GB, Windows 11), one build takes 17.3–17.8 s and make repro 33.7 s. The label-free test B is reproduced by tools/readjudicate.py, and the DFL evidence by tools/dead_ball_v2.py dfl-bench, from data downloaded outside the repository.
